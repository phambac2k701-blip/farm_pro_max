# CANON STORY — v1

## Central premise
An, a 20-year-old university freshman and former student of Minh Khai High School, receives a message from his missing friend Khang at 00:17: **“Tao tìm thấy người thứ chín.”** The attached image shows a school-radio group of eight named students and a ninth figure cropped by the frame. Khang claims the missing figure is not the same person across the archive.

An enters the school at night to find Khang and the source of the inconsistency. As An compares physical spaces, photographs, notebooks, broadcasts, and administrative records, the environment begins to acquire a ninth place, object, voice, or occupant corresponding to knowledge An has just learned.

## The actual past event
In 2012, Minh Khai High School ran an internal night radio service during the school festival. Eight students were officially assigned to the radio team. Because someone had to handle after-hours recording, emergency notes, and microphone control while teachers were absent, an informal ninth duty was created. The supervising teacher and student committee used an obsolete student identifier, **09**, to label the work. The identifier was convenient because it already existed in paper templates and did not immediately map to a current student.

On the night of 12 October 2012 at approximately 00:17, a student named Phương was injured in a dark stairwell while returning equipment. The unlisted duty holder, Minh, recorded what happened and attempted to make the event part of the school broadcast log. The teacher stopped the broadcast and instructed the team to keep the incident “out of the program.” No one maliciously planned a disappearance; the institution chose a clean administrative version of the night.

To close the paperwork, the school distributed parts of the work among the eight official members and retained the “09” slot in some templates. Over the next years, whoever informally did the same after-hours work was sometimes tagged with 09. Old photos, notes, and audio therefore contain different human beings in the Ninth position.

## Why the records contradict
There are four layers:
1. Official documents preserve the eight-member team because responsibility was assignable.
2. Personal notebooks preserve the actual people who performed the Ninth duty.
3. Photos preserve whoever physically occupied the Ninth position on a given date.
4. Reused archive templates preserve 09 as if it were a stable person.

The contradiction is therefore real, but each artifact is locally truthful.

## Why memories contradict
Students remember “the Ninth” as a person because role and person became fused through repetition. People remember the colleague who occupied the role during their own year. Their memories are not fabricated, but they are incomplete and role-shaped.

## The missing friend
Khang begins with the wrong hypothesis: there was one erased student named “the Ninth.” He discovers that three people from different years were independently remembered as the Ninth. He then finds a 2012 audio fragment with the same 00:17 timestamp in a folder labeled 09. After entering the archive room alone, his phone starts receiving messages from an account that should not exist. He leaves behind the message that brings An to the school.

Khang is not dead. He is alive but trapped in an unstable “ninth-state” inside the old radio/archive route: the school’s physical and documentary logic keeps treating him as the current bearer of the role because he is the person most directly reconstructing it. He can communicate intermittently through devices already linked to the archive.

## Protagonist arc
An starts with a practical goal: find Khang. He also carries a small moral failure: when Khang previously raised the 09 inconsistency, An told him to stop obsessing over old school gossip. An begins the game valuing present relationships over uncomfortable records. By the end, he must choose whether preserving a clean life story is more important than preserving the evidence of people whose names were made inconvenient.

## Antagonistic force
The main human opposition is **Vân**, 44, the school’s current records coordinator and former student-radio advisor. She does not believe in a supernatural “ninth.” Her goal is to prevent the old incident from being misrepresented during the school anniversary digitization. She removes documents, redirects requests, and pressures current students not to repeat rumor. Her fear is not personal evil but that a messy partial record will be treated as a proven accusation against people who are gone.

Vân becomes the human face of the theme because she is partly right: an archive can hurt people when evidence is incomplete. Her mistake is believing that deleting ambiguity is the same as preserving truth.

## The Ninth
The Ninth is canonical as a **role**, not a single identity. The role carries four functions:
- after-hours operator;
- unofficial witness;
- holder of evidence that does not fit the official record;
- person expected to absorb responsibility without formal attribution.

Different occupants can honestly be called “the Ninth.”

## KCR
KCR is the phenomenon by which a coherent set of evidence about an omitted role changes what linked parts of the world can consistently represent. The operational rule is:

**When An has gathered sufficient cross-source evidence to establish one previously unacknowledged Ninth-state, a linked physical, archival, audio, device, or representational layer resolves toward the most coherent state that includes that knowledge.**

A KCR event may therefore alter geometry, an object arrangement, an archive index, a device state, a reflection, or another authored representation. The exact manifestation is defined per event and must never contradict the canonical timeline.

The game does not state this rule to the player. It is inferred through repetition.

The exact ontology remains ambiguous. The game allows a supernatural reading, a collective-memory reading, or a hybrid. What is not ambiguous is that KCR is state-driven, deterministic, and narratively tied to evidence.

## Major midpoint reveal
The player proves there was not one missing person. The same 09 role was occupied by multiple people across different years. This recontextualizes the apparently contradictory photos.

## Late reveal
Khang was not merely researching the Ninth. By reconstructing the role, he became the current bearer of it. His disappearance is a consequence of the same mechanism the player is now activating.

## Final reframe
The real horror is not that a ghost impersonates different students. The institution created an unaccountable empty position, and people kept filling it. Once the player forces the role into visibility, reality tries to complete the missing slot with whoever has become its strongest witness.
