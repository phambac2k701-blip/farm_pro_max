## Core premise
The protagonist is a student whose friend disappears after investigating an inconsistency hidden across school records, photographs, audio, rooms, and memories.

A strange message/link left by the missing friend contains the phrase:

> Tao tìm thấy người thứ chín.

An old class image appears to contain nine people, yet official records and living memory consistently account for only eight. Across different sources, the identity or appearance of the ninth figure is unstable.

## Narrative promise
The player must feel that:
- the mystery is investigable, not random;
- clues connect across chapters;
- earlier scenes gain new meaning later;
- reality can change because the player learned something;
- uncertainty exists, but the game is not meaningless ambiguity.

## Themes
- memory versus record
- absence
- unreliable institutions/archives
- the fear of being forgotten
- whether evidence creates truth or merely reveals it
- ordinary places becoming unfamiliar

## Mystery rules
1. Every major reveal must have prior evidence or foreshadowing.
2. Contradictions must be intentional and traceable.
3. A reality shift must be caused by a known state/evidence condition.
4. The player should be able to reconstruct a coherent core timeline by the end.
5. Supernatural/psychological ambiguity may remain around interpretation, but not around every basic fact.
6. Do not use arbitrary “it was all a dream” reversals.
7. Do not invalidate player investigation merely for a twist.

## Truth layers
Narrative information is tracked in three layers:

### Observed
What the player directly sees/hears in the current world state.

### Recorded
Documents, photos, audio, logs, files, and testimony.

### Underlying
The internal canonical event/state used by the game’s narrative system.

Observed and Recorded may conflict. Underlying truth should remain internally consistent even if not all of it is revealed.

## Core mechanic relationship
**Knowledge Changes Reality** is narrative, not decorative.

Example:
- initial classroom contains eight desks;
- player discovers evidence supporting a ninth seat;
- after a controlled revisit, the classroom contains nine desks;
- the new desk exposes a notebook/evidence path;
- the game never announces “the world changed.”

## Investigation discipline
Every critical clue should define:
- source
- chapter introduced
- what it appears to mean
- what it actually establishes
- related clues
- world-state effect
- later reinterpretation, if any

## Working 9-chapter arc
1. **Tin nhắn 00:17** — disappearance and first impossible clue.
2. **Phòng phát thanh** — audio contradicts official memory.
3. **Chiếc bàn thứ chín** — first undeniable reality shift.
4. **Tấm ảnh** — multiple incompatible copies of the same image.
5. **Chuyến xe cuối** — investigation leaves school; timeline expands.
6. **Căn phòng thuê** — the missing friend’s prior investigation is uncovered.
7. **Hồ sơ năm cũ** — current events connect to an older case.
8. **Đêm đó** — player reconstructs the contradictory timeline.
9. **Người thứ chín** — final synthesis and interpretation.

Chapter names and exact contents are working material, not immutable shipping copy.

## Chapter reveal rule
Each chapter must do at least one of:
- answer one existing question;
- create one stronger question;
- reinterpret an earlier clue;
- alter a previously explored place;
- connect two previously separate evidence threads.

Prefer doing two or more.

## Tone
Quiet, investigative, lonely, familiar, unsettling.

Avoid:
- excessive gore
- constant jumpscares
- exposition dumps
- villain monologues explaining every mystery
- random visual corruption with no narrative function

## Character policy
Do not lock names/backgrounds until they matter to clue logic.

Current required roles:
- protagonist/player
- missing friend
- people visible/associated with the class record
- witnesses or record-owners introduced only when needed
- the “ninth” identity/concept

When a character becomes production-critical, document:
- public identity
- relationship to protagonist
- what they know
- what they believe
- what they hide/misremember
- chapter appearances
- evidence they create or contradict

## Continuity requirement
Any new plot element that affects another chapter must be added here or to chapter-specific docs before implementation.

Narrative continuity is reviewed alongside code continuity.
